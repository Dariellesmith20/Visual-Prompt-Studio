VISUAL PROMPT #1
Historical Theme: Art and the Afterlife
Historical Period: Ancient Egypt
Medium: Funerary mask (sculpture)
Visual Prompt:
This artwork shows a gold funerary mask of an Egyptian pharaoh. The face looks very calm and perfect, with smooth skin and no real emotion.
The eyes are shaped like almonds and outlined in black. The headdress has blue and black stripes, and there are detailed patterns all over the mask.
It looks very rich and important. In the background, you can see the inside of a tomb with hieroglyphics on the walls and a warm golden glow. Everything looks balanced and centered, which makes it feel stable and powerful.

Historical Purpose (Why):
This kind of artwork was made because
Egyptians strongly believed in the afterlife. They thought the body needed to be preserved so the person could live again after death. The mask helped protect the person and made sure their spirit could recognize them. The gold shows that the pharaoh was seen as a god or very powerful.

 VISUAL PROMPT #2
Historical Theme: Humanism and the move toward science
Historical Period: Early Renaissance
Medium: Painting (fresco)
Visual Prompt:
This artwork is a realistic painting of people standing inside a building with arches and columns. The people look more natural, not stiff, and you can see emotion in their faces and movement in their bodies. There is one main figure in the center, and the space behind them looks deep because of perspective. The artist uses light and shadows to make everything look more 3D. The colors are soft and realistic, and the lines lead your eyes into the background.



Historical Purpose (Why):
This artwork shows how artists during the Renaissance started focusing more on people and real life. Instead of just making religious symbols, they wanted things to look realistic.
They also started using math and science, like perspective, to make their work more accurate.
This connects to humanism, where people cared more about learning, observation, and human experience. It shows a big shift from the Middle Ages to a more modern way of thinking.
