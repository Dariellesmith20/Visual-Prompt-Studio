Prompt Part A: Create a watercolor painting of a peaceful forest at sunrise reflected in a calm lake, focusing on line, color, contrast, movement, texture, and unity to shape the composition. Using round sable brushes for detail and a flat wash brush for wider areas on cold-pressed watercolor paper, begin with a wet-on-wet wash to lay in the glowing sky, letting the warm golds and soft pinks gently blend and bleed into cooler blues. Allow the paint to flow naturally, creating soft, diffused edges in the distant trees so they feel slightly misty and full of atmosphere. Slowly build up transparent layers through glazing to deepen the shadows in the foreground, increasing contrast and creating emphasis with darker vertical tree trunks that draw the viewer’s eye upward. Add light dry-brush touches to suggest the rough texture of bark and scattered foliage. Let the reflection in the lake softly mirror the trees and sky, keeping the colors harmonious so the whole scene feels unified, airy, and luminous—clearly embracing watercolor’s fluid movement, transparency, and delicate layering.
Promt Part B: Create a linocut print of a peaceful forest at sunrise reflected in a still lake, keeping the same overall composition but translating it into bold, graphic forms. Focus on line, shape, contrast, movement, texture, and unity to organize the design. Begin by sketching the scene onto a linoleum block, then use V- and U-shaped gouges to carefully carve away the negative space through an incising, subtractive process. Let the tall tree trunks be defined by strong, carved vertical lines to create movement and natural emphasis, drawing the viewer’s eye upward. Simplify the sunrise and its reflection into clear, high-contrast shapes, allowing the light areas to remain uncarved so they print brightly against the darker inked surfaces. Use repeated carved marks to suggest texture in the bark and subtle ripples in the water. After rolling oil-based relief ink onto the raised surface with a brayer, press the block onto paper using a printing press or hand burnishing. The finished print should highlight linocut’s distinctive qualities—crisp edges, bold contrasts, tactile carved textures, and expressive, hand-cut lines—while maintaining a sense of unity throughout the composition.
